The Ashcombe Valley Observer

The season that moved

Thirty-four years of readings from the town weir say the valley's wet season now begins about four weeks later than it did in the early 1990s, and lands harder when it comes.

From the weir record, 1990 to 2023. Three missing seasons are shown as the gaps they are.

The short version

In the first five recorded years, 1990-1994, the wet season opened around September 9 on average. In the last five, 2018 and 2020-2023, it opened around October 7: a shift of 28 days. The seasons also grew wetter, from a mean of 606mm to 692mm, a rise of 86mm, about 14%. The heaviest single days grew too, from a mean of 43mm to 72mm.

That is the whole story. The charts and the table below only show you the same record, year by year.

The season starts later

Ask an Ashcombe gardener when the rain comes and the older answer is early September; the weir keeper's 1994 log has the meadow cut in August and the rain still holding off at fair time. The weir puts numbers to the memory. Each year's mark below is the day the record first opens a wet ten-day window; the marks walk down the page toward October as the years pass.

Two kinds of reading sit in that picture. Until 1996 the gauge was read by hand, twice a day; from 1997 the weir station logged itself. The dotted line marks the change, and comparisons across it should be read with that in mind.

And lands harder

The later season is not a smaller one. Season totals have climbed as the start has slipped, and the missing seasons are drawn as missing rather than bridged.

The hardest day in the book

The weir has one entry unlike the others. In the October 2019 storm the station logged nearly three times the heaviest day the manual gauge ever recorded.

VERIFIED

132mm

The largest 24-hour fall in the record, logged at the weir in the storm of October 12, 2019. Season total that year: 702mm, onset day 275.

Nothing since has approached it, but the ordinary heavy days have kept climbing all the same.

About this record. The Ashcombe Valley series runs 1990 to 2023: 31 recorded seasons and 3 the instruments missed. Readings to 1996 come from a manual gauge read twice daily; from 1997, from the automated weir station. Comparisons that cross that boundary are labeled with both methods, here and in the charts.

This record describes one valley's weir. It describes the shift, not its cause; no attribution analysis exists in this data, and none is offered.

The Ashcombe Valley Observer is a fictional paper; this page is a design study.
