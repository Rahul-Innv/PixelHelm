The Season That Moved

The town weir’s ledger, printed in two inks: what the manual gauge saw, and what the automated weir sees now.

umber — the manual-gauge years, 1990–1996  ·  blue — the weir years, 1997–2023. The ink changes with the instrument, so every comparison carries its caveat with it.

The valley’s wet season now begins about four weeks later than in the early 1990s, and it lands harder. In the gauge years 1990–1994 the first sustained rain arrived around September 9 (day 252); across the verified weir years 2018 and 2020–2023 it arrived around October 7 (day 280) — 28 days later. A season that once brought about 606 mm now brings about 692 mm (up 86 mm, or 14%), and the heaviest single day rose from about 43 mm to about 72 mm.

Those two figures print in two inks on purpose: the instrument changed after 1996, so every then-and-now comparison on this page crosses from the manual gauge to the automated weir. Three years are missing, one 2019 reading is unverified, and the page describes the shift in this one valley’s record, not its cause.

About this record

What counts as the season’s start. The weir’s working definition: the first day of the water year (September 1 is day 244) that opens the first 10-day window with at least 25 mm of rain in total. The season’s total runs from that onset through the last qualifying 10-day window; the heaviest day is the largest 24-hour rainfall inside the season.

Why two inks. From 1990 through 1996 the readings came from a manual gauge, read twice daily; from 1997 the automated weir station took over. Rather than bury that in a footnote, this page prints each era in its own ink, so the caveat travels with every figure.

Three years are missing; one reading is unverified. 1998 (gauge failure) and 2003–2004 (funding lapsed) are drawn as open gaps and excluded from every average. 2019’s heaviest-day reading of 132 mm is unverified and 2019 is left out of the recent-era averages.

What this page can and cannot say. This record describes one valley’s weir. It contains no attribution analysis: the page describes the shift, not its cause.
