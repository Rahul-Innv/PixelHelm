How to read it

A wet season here is not a date on a calendar. It opens on the first day of a ten-day window that brings at least 25mm of rain, and it closes with the last window that still qualifies. Everything below is counted that way, season after season, so the lines can be set beside one another.

  • Onset dayThe first day of the water year, counting Sep 1 as day 244, that opens the first ten-day window with at least 25mm of rainfall.
  • Season totalTotal rainfall from onset through the last qualifying ten-day window.
  • Peak fallThe largest 24-hour rainfall inside the wet season.

The instrument changed partway through. Readings from 1990 to 1996 come from the manual gauge, read twice daily by the keeper. From 1997 onward they come from the automated weir station. Any comparison that crosses 1996 and 1997 is crossing that change, and this page says so wherever it makes one.

What moved

Read down the onset column and the season starts later. It opened on day 251 in 1990 and on day 281 in 2023. Read down the totals and they are larger at the end than at the beginning: 612 mm in 1990, 715 mm in 2023. The two readings sit either side of the instrument change, so that comparison crosses the manual gauge and the automated weir, and the change of instrument is part of what it contains.

The gaps are part of the shape too. Three seasons have no record here, and they are printed as blank lines rather than skipped, because a record that closes over its own holes reads smoother than it deserves to.

This page describes the shift in one valley's weir record and not its cause. There is no attribution analysis in the underlying record, so there is none here either.
