# The false-positive filter — distinctive ≠ defect

Source: Dammyjay93 interface-design `design-review.md` ("taste ≠ defect; a
bold-but-intended choice is ratified by the system, not flagged") and its
"What is NOT slop" guard ("it does not become good by defaulting harder; if unsure
whether something is a tell or a decision, treat it as a decision").

This is the single most important guard on the panel. Without it, a critique loop
quietly flattens every motivated choice into a safe default — which manufactures the
exact homogeneity the council exists to fight. Apply it BEFORE flagging anything.

## The rule

> A bold, motivated choice — a saturated palette, a dramatic type scale, a signature
> motion moment, an unconventional layout — is a **SUCCESS**, not a violation.
> Auto-flagging it is the anti-pattern. When unsure whether something is a tell or a
> decision, **treat it as a decision.**

## How to apply it

For any element a lens wants to flag, ask in order:

1. **Is it motivated by the brief/subject?** A loud palette on a product whose world
   is loud is correct. A loud palette on a calm-trust register is a defect. Judge
   against the active profile's register, never a global "safe" ideal.
2. **Is it consistent / ratified by the system?** A deliberate choice repeats with
   intent across the design. A one-off accident does not. Repetition with purpose →
   decision; lonely oddity → possible tell.
3. **Does it survive the exit tests?** A bold choice that PASSES Swap/Squint/Signature
   is intentional craft. A bold choice that FAILS them is just noise wearing boldness.

If it passes 1–2 (and ideally 3), it is a decision — do NOT flag it; if anything,
name it as a strength to keep.

## The other direction

The filter protects boldness; it does NOT excuse slop. Defaulting harder is not a
choice:

- "Minimal" is only a decision if the spacing, type, and detail are precise. A flat,
  under-considered page is not minimalism — it's absence. Minimal directions are held
  to HIGHER micro-craft scrutiny, not lower.
- A safe, generic, category-default look does not become good because it's "clean."
  Quiet competence still fails the Swap and Signature tests.

## Net effect on the verdict

- Never penalize a direction for being distinctive.
- Never reward a direction for being safe.
- Penalize only: measured floor failures (Layer-1, already eliminated), genuine
  usability/honesty defects, and detail-level slop that fails the exit tests.
