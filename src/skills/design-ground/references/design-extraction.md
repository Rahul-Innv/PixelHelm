# Design extraction — reading a reference's DNA to ground an arm that beats it

The highest-altitude grounding move: point at a reference SITE you (or the owner) love — a
north-star product, a best-in-class competitor, an award-winner — **extract its full design DNA**,
ground ONE tournament arm on that DNA, and beat it on the project's own register + honesty floor.

This is [[L-037]] (a tournament that only clears the *incumbent's* altitude can still miss the
owner's absolute bar) turned into a repeatable step, and it complements the reference-source
registry (`reference-sources.md`): the registry feeds trend VOCABULARY and canon; extraction reads
ONE specific artifact's whole system. It is an OPTIONAL enrichment — grounding completes without it.

## THE LAW (identical to the registry seam — extraction is NOT cloning)

- **Extract SYSTEM QUALITIES, never hex, never pixels, never shots, never copy.** The project's
  own token contract supplies every value. You take the *shape* of the system (how the ramp is
  built, what the scale's ratio is, where the eye is led), never a single lifted number or asset.
  A lifted hex or a copied layout is a defect, not a strong extraction.
- **The reference sets the CEILING to clear, never the design to ship.** The output is an altitude
  bar for one arm to beat, not a target to reproduce. A faithful clone fails the honesty gate and
  the "would another AI arrive here?" falsifiability bar just as hard as a template does.
- **Real data and real copy are NEVER lifted.** The reference's numbers, names, and words stay the
  reference's; the project's own real data fills the generated design (no concept-art, no fake
  figures — the honesty/fabrication gate is unchanged).

## The DNA — six dimensions to read (as words + ratios, never values)

1. **Token / color ramp** — the color-ROLE architecture: neutral-ladder shape (how many steps,
   warm/cool cast), accent strategy (single restrained vs multi), semantic-role encoding (how
   state/meaning maps to color). Take the ramp's SHAPE → re-derive it in the project's own ramp
   (OKLCH-considered), never the hexes.
2. **Type scale** — the modular ratio and step count, display-vs-body size contrast, weight
   strategy, tracking on display sizes. Take the SCALE, not the px.
3. **Space rhythm** — the base unit + modular scale, density register (generous vs compact),
   section-to-section rhythm. Take the ratios and the register, not the pixel gaps.
4. **Composition / anatomy** — grid strategy, hierarchy pattern (hero-led / F-or-Z scan / ledger /
   editorial), how the eye is led to the one primary action, and the load-bearing component's
   anatomy (slots, states). Take the PATTERN, not the markup.
5. **Motion** — the timing register (snappy vs calm), easing family, what animates vs what
   deliberately holds still, entrance choreography. Take the vocabulary, not the keyframes; the
   reduced-motion contract is a floor, not a style.
6. **A11y contracts** — the keyboard/focus/state model implied by the anatomy (APG is the floor for
   the generated arm regardless of what the reference does).

## The method (how to extract honestly)

1. **Get the reference in front of your eyes — you cannot read DNA from an agent fetch.** A text
   fetch is image-blind ([[L-042]]): it yields the hex/pixels you must NOT lift and none of the
   system qualities your eye reads. So either **render the real page** (`design-render` on the URL →
   screenshots, both modes where the site supports it — this is the same render-real-competitor-pages
   machinery) OR use **owner-supplied screenshots** (the "human-supplied-screenshot tier" — see
   `reference-sources.md`; this is how the gallery genre, Godly/Awwwards/Land-book/Mobbin/Magic-UI,
   legitimately enters, since they fail agent-fetch but a human can hand you the shot).
2. **Read the DNA off the RENDER, not the source.** The render gives you the system qualities; the
   HTML/CSS gives you liftable values you are forbidden to take. Read with your eyes.
3. **Write the extracted DNA as a brief** (DESIGN.md dialect): one paragraph per dimension, in words
   + ratios, with an explicit **red-lines line** naming what you deliberately did NOT take (the
   brand payload, the typeface, the exact palette, any proprietary identity). This brief folds into
   the ground context as an `extractionBrief`, consumed downstream like the other grounding inputs.

## How it feeds the loop (without homogenizing it — [[L-040]])

- The extracted DNA grounds **exactly ONE arm** — the north-star arm (or, in the full edition, the
  external-engine arm's ground). **Do not hand the DNA to every arm** — a shared rich reference
  collapses distinct engines onto one attractor (that is exactly the L-040 failure). The other arms
  keep their own divergence axes; the chair's pre-render convergence check still applies.
- That arm's mandate: **match the reference's altitude, then beat it** on the project's register and
  honesty floor. The reference raises the bar the field must clear; it never becomes the answer.
- **Register-fit + the incumbent guard still decide.** A gorgeous extraction that is off-register
  loses ([[L-012]]). Extraction changes the *ceiling*, never the *gate*.

## When to use / not

- **USE** when the owner's words name an absolute bar ("like the best X", "humanly innovative",
  "really good dashboards") or the owner points at a specific site; on a real redesign that must leap
  altitude, not merely beat a weak incumbent.
- **DON'T** use it as a stand-in for the register (it is a ceiling input, not the target); to clone a
  competitor (honesty gate); or on a quick pass (it costs a render + a brief — spend it when the
  altitude actually matters).
